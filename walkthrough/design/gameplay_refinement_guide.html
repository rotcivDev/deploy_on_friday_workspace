Gameplay Backlog Refinement Guide: v0.1 Prototype

This document evaluates the gameplay and content design impact of walkthrough/architecture/kickoff_backlog.html and refines the game-design work into increments for the v0.1 Root Prototype.

This is a mentorship and support artifact. It does not require agents to edit production source directories.

1. Backlog Review Summary

Second Review: Source-of-Truth Alignment. Gameplay design is the clearest expression of the domain language in v0.1. Card names, encounter bands, bot personalities, curse states, rewards, and playtest metrics should drive implementation terminology. Do not add tactical structures or content abstractions until the rule, player-facing meaning, and playtest signal are clear.

The kickoff backlog has the right playable target: one human, two deterministic bots, seeded cards, visible logs, and a complete race from Level 1 to Level 10. The design risk is that the first playable slice could become technically complete but not meaningfully playable if the content curve is not constrained.

The current v0.1 card set is funny and on-theme, but it is not yet a balanced progression set:

  • Early characters start around 3 to 5 Power: Level 1 plus class base bonus.
  • Null Pointer Exception at Power 2 is the only guaranteed early win.
  • Legacy Monolith at Power 10, AI threats at Power 12 to 22, Production Outage at Power 22, and The Singularity at Power 50 are mostly midgame or scripted-test threats.
  • Starting with two Treasure cards may accelerate Power if the player draws equipment immediately, but the game still needs deterministic fixture decks that prove progression without relying on luck.

The design refinement should therefore add clear balance bands, scripted validation scenarios, bot behavior limits, and playtest metrics before adding more card categories or class abilities.

2. v0.1 Gameplay Scope

In Scope

  • A deterministic three-player local match: one human and two bots.
  • A Level 1 to Level 10 progression race.
  • Encounter cards: monsters, AI threats treated as monster-like threats, and immediate curses.
  • Treasure rewards: equipment cards with persistent Power bonuses.
  • Simple help and sabotage modifiers.
  • Short, readable turn logs with satirical software-engineering tone.
  • Minimal class differentiation through base Power only.
  • Fixed-seed playtest scripts that prove early, mid, late, win, and loss scenarios.

Out Of Scope For v0.1

  • Full PvP card play.
  • Trading and negotiation economies.
  • Equipment slot limits.
  • Deep Influence interactions.
  • Class-specific active abilities.
  • AI chain reactions or multi-card combo effects.
  • Large expansion content.
  • Persistent match achievements and rankings.

3. Backlog Alignment Findings

Finding 1: R2 Needs A Designed Encounter Curve

R2 defines the Encounter deck, but the current candidates need balance bands so deterministic tests do not repeatedly produce unwinnable early turns.

Recommended v0.1 encounter bands:

Band Power Purpose Current Cards
Tutorial 1-5 First wins, first reward draw, first combat log Null Pointer Exception
Early 6-9 Pressure after one equipment card Needs 1-2 more support cards or scripted low-power variants
Mid 10-15 First meaningful help requests Legacy Monolith, Hallucinated Function, Infinite Agent Loop
Late 16-25 Requires equipment, level gains, or help Production Outage, Autonomous Junior Developer
Boss 40+ Scripted danger or rare endgame spike The Singularity

Design recommendation:

  • Keep The Singularity rare or scripted.
  • Do not let the early shuffled deck contain too many Power 12+ threats before the player has equipment.
  • Add low-power support encounters in a later content increment if the first playtest stalls.

Finding 2: R3 Needs Treasure Pacing Targets

R3 correctly uses equipment as Treasure, but it needs a pacing rule so Power growth is visible without making every fight trivial.

Recommended v0.1 equipment curve:

Card Power Bonus Design Role
ThinkPad +2 First visible upgrade
Linux Mastery +3 Early-mid stabilizer
PostgreSQL Expertise +4 Midgame enabler
Emacs Mastery +6 Late swing item

Design recommendation:

  • Starting Treasure cards are acceptable for v0.1, but tests should also cover a no-equipment early fight.
  • Do not add equipment slots yet.
  • If every bot equips Emacs Mastery too early in fixed-seed simulations, reduce its deck quantity instead of adding a complex slot system.

Finding 3: R4 Needs A Default Bad-Stuff Table

R4 specifies a deterministic fallback penalty: lose 1 Level, minimum Level 1. That is good, but named high-impact cards should have readable bad stuff in the design guide even if the first implementation uses fallback behavior.

Recommended v0.1 bad stuff:

Encounter Bad Stuff
Null Pointer Exception Lose 1 Level, minimum Level 1
Legacy Monolith Lose 1 Level and discard 1 random card if available
Production Outage Lose 2 Levels, minimum Level 1
Hallucinated Function Discard 1 Treasure card from hand if available
Infinite Agent Loop Skip the next Play/Equip step
Autonomous Junior Developer Discard 1 equipped item if available, otherwise lose 1 Level
The Singularity Lose 3 Levels, minimum Level 1

Implementation can still begin with the fallback penalty, but the backlog should reserve these named effects as the first balance refinement after the combat resolver works.

Finding 4: R5 Curse Effects Are Good But Need Expiry Rules

The three seeded curses are appropriate for the first prototype. The important refinement is to make their lifetime explicit.

Recommended curse state:

Curse State Expiry
Broken CI skip_next_combat = true Clears after the next revealed monster or AI threat is discarded
Scope Creep next_monster_power_bonus += 5 Clears after the next combat starts
Context Window Exceeded No lasting state Resolves immediately

Design recommendation:

  • Do not stack Broken CI; drawing it again while active should refresh the same flag.
  • Scope Creep can stack only if the implementation already supports additive modifiers cleanly. Otherwise, cap it at +5 for v0.1.

Finding 5: R6 Bots Need Personality Rules, Not Intelligence

The backlog already says bots should be predictable. The design layer should make each bot's behavior distinct enough to create useful playtests without adding complex AI.

Recommended v0.1 bots:

Bot Personality Equip Rule Help Rule Sabotage Rule
Legacy Maintainer Defensive, conservative Equip highest Power item Helps if active player is losing by 2 or less Sabotages only if human would reach Level 10
Prompt Intern Chaotic, opportunistic Equip first available item Helps if it gains Treasure from the deal Sabotages if human would win by 2 or less

Design recommendation:

  • Use deterministic thresholds and seeded tie-breakers.
  • Keep bot choices explainable in one log sentence.
  • Do not require bots to understand every future card type.

4. Increment Plan

GD0. Seeded Content Design Audit

Outcome: confirm that seeded content supports a playable Level 1 to Level 10 race.

Scope:

  • Review current monster, AI, curse, and equipment cards.
  • Assign every encounter to a power band.
  • Identify missing early-game support cards.
  • Flag any card that should be rare, scripted, or deferred.

Acceptance criteria:

  • Every v0.1 card has a design role.
  • The Singularity is marked rare or scripted.
  • At least one fixed-seed scenario can produce an early player win.

Verification:

  • Balance table review against seed data.
  • Manual review of R2 and R3 deck quantities.

GD1. Encounter And Treasure Pacing

Outcome: make the first shuffled decks produce testable progression.

Scope:

  • Define Encounter deck quantity targets by power band.
  • Define Treasure deck quantity targets by equipment bonus.
  • Keep early progression possible without needing complex class abilities.
  • Create one "scripted tutorial fixture" draw order for deterministic tests.

Acceptance criteria:

  • The first three turns in the tutorial fixture include at least one beatable encounter.
  • A player can gain Treasure before facing Power 12+ threats in at least one fixed-seed test.
  • Boss-tier threats do not dominate early playtests.

Verification:

  • Fixed-seed deck transcript review.
  • Full match simulation target: one match ends before turn 40.

GD2. Combat And Reward Balance

Outcome: make combat outcomes readable, fair, and quick enough for terminal play.

Scope:

  • Lock v0.1 combat rule: player Power greater than or equal to monster Power wins.
  • Use fallback bad stuff first, then named bad stuff as the next refinement.
  • Confirm reward levels and reward treasures create steady Level 10 pressure.

Acceptance criteria:

  • A win clearly grants configured levels and treasure draws.
  • A loss never drops Level below 1.
  • A Level 10 reward ends the match immediately.
  • Named bad-stuff effects are documented even if not implemented in the first resolver.

Verification:

  • Scripted win, loss, and Level 10 tests.
  • Manual playtest notes for whether turns feel stalled.

GD3. Curse And Temporary Modifier Rules

Outcome: keep setbacks clear without creating hidden state.

Scope:

  • Define expiry rules for every seeded curse.
  • Define how temporary monster bonuses appear in logs and snapshots.
  • Prevent long-lived penalty stacks from taking over v0.1.

Acceptance criteria:

  • Each curse can be explained in one sentence.
  • Each curse has an explicit expiry.
  • The UI log can display curse resolution without inspecting private rule state.

Verification:

  • Unit test design notes for each curse.
  • Playtest checklist for visible curse logs.

GD4. Bot Behavior Baseline

Outcome: make local solo tests exercise help and sabotage without unpredictable bot play.

Scope:

  • Assign Legacy Maintainer and Prompt Intern personality rules.
  • Define deterministic equip, help, and sabotage thresholds.
  • Keep all bot actions valid through the same command boundary as human actions.

Acceptance criteria:

  • Bots equip available items.
  • Bots ask for help only under a simple threshold.
  • Bots can sabotage the human in a visible, bounded way.
  • Replaying the same seed produces the same bot decisions.

Verification:

  • Full-match simulation transcript.
  • Log review for bot action clarity.

GD5. Terminal Playtest Script

Outcome: make the first manual playtest evaluate game feel, not just whether the program runs.

Scope:

  • Define a short checklist for a human playtester.
  • Track progression speed, confusing logs, dead turns, and runaway leaders.
  • Capture which cards felt too weak, too punishing, or too frequent.

Acceptance criteria:

  • A tester can understand the current phase and available action from the terminal state.
  • A tester sees at least one combat win, one loss or curse, one equipment play, one bot action, and one Level gain within the first 10 turns.
  • A match can complete without adding new rules mid-playtest.

Verification:

  • Manual playtest notes with seed, winner, turn count, and balance observations.

5. Suggested v0.1 Balance Targets

Use these as review targets, not hard production rules:

Metric Target
Match length 20 to 40 total turns
First player Level gain By turn 3 to 5
First equipment play By turn 1 to 3
First help or sabotage event By turn 8 to 15
Average early encounter Power 2 to 8
Midgame encounter Power 10 to 15
Late encounter Power 16 to 25
Boss encounter frequency Rare or scripted
Dead turns No more than 2 in a row for the human

If a fixed-seed match misses these targets, adjust content quantities or scripted fixtures before adding new mechanics.

7. Log Tone Guide

Logs should be short, concrete, and funny because the terminal UI has limited room.

Recommended style:


Legacy Maintainer equipped PostgreSQL Expertise. The schema has opinions now.
Prompt Intern sabotaged the fight. The monster gained +2 Power from "one small prompt tweak."
Broken CI resolved. Your next combat gets skipped while everyone blames YAML.
Scope Creep resolved. The next monster gains +5 Power and a steering committee.
Context Window Exceeded resolved. Half your hand vanished into summarization.

Avoid long jokes in logs. Put longer jokes in card descriptions instead.
