Market Research Response

Cross-mentor evaluation of MARKET_RESEARCH.txt and resulting product documentation changes.

Executive Decision

Continue the product, but reposition it. Deploy on Friday should not present itself as a derivative tabletop-card-game adaptation. The ownable product promise is: a terminal-native multiplayer incident-management satire where developers weaponize bugs, tools, influence, tech debt, and production chaos to become Principal Engineer.

The research says the project has a strong niche, high community potential, medium commercial upside, and real differentiation if it leans into developer identity, terminal-first presentation, modular extensions, and signature software-culture mechanics.

Positioning Changes

  • Use: "terminal-native multiplayer incident-management satire."
  • Use: "developers weaponize bugs, tools, influence, tech debt, and production chaos."
  • Lead public brand emphasis with incident response plus terminal culture.
  • Use workplace satire as the tone and software-history eras as expansion flavor.
  • Avoid public wording that compares the product to named existing tabletop brands.
  • Make the terminal UI an identity feature, not just a low-budget interface.
  • Make match transcripts and replay logs part of the shareable community loop.

Recommended Roadmap Adjustment

  1. v0.1: default deck, deterministic local loop, bots, terminal smoke path, replay log, 3-match daily online quota plan, abandoned-match cooldown policy, and Incident Severity as the signature mechanic.
  2. v0.2: modular content foundation with main deck represented as the first content pack.
  3. v0.3: unlocks and match setup flow, with copy-paste invite support or clear private-server setup.
  4. v0.4: mixed-theme alpha using two extensions and fixed-seed balance transcripts.
  5. Paid early access gate: no paid build until official online multiplayer exists.
  6. v1.0: polished main deck, official deck recipes, public demo, stable multiplayer, and extension-ready catalog.

Clarification Questions

These need owner decisions before the next roadmap rewrite.
  1. Decided: first public artifact is a free demo with the default deck, local play with bots, and 3 completed online matches per account per day.
  2. Decided: daily-limited multiplayer means a quota of 3 completed online matches per player/account per day, not a scheduled time window or rotating room.
  3. Decided: only completed online matches consume daily quota; repeated abandons or disconnects trigger a temporary cooldown.
  4. Decided: do not charge for early access until official online multiplayer exists. Local/bot play and private server support can exist earlier, but they stay in the free demo/playtest path.
  5. Decided: Incident Severity defines v0.1. Influence economy, Shared Tech Debt, Deployment Windows, and deeper Career Identity builds are deferred until Incident Severity is playable and testable.
  6. Decided: extensions use paid ownership plus in-game unlock progression. Paid ownership grants access to a theme; gameplay progression unlocks recipes, cosmetics, achievements, and advanced variants inside owned content. Do not sell strictly stronger cards.
  7. Decided: public brand emphasis leads with incident response plus terminal culture. Workplace satire is the tone; software-history eras are expansion flavor.
  8. Decided: Deploy on Friday is the public product title. Code and infrastructure examples must not use product-title-derived prefixes; current examples use game_.
